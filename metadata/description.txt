Boustrophedon is a daily reading practice for National Gallery captions, kept on this device.

Home opens on today. Five stops are already cut:

1. Bench spells a word you missed.
2. Plough walks the caption the way an ox turns: left to right, then back from the right.
3. Face taps every backward word until the line reads forward.
4. Route opens the next painting on your visit list.
5. Recall spells a word from your book.

Finish all five and today is filed. Tomorrow is cut from what you missed, what is still on the route, and what you pinned.

A miss does not disappear. That word is due again tomorrow. A clean face waits longer: one day, then two, four, eight, and sixteen. Due today is counted on the home card, so you can see what you still owe.

The library on this device holds fifty National Gallery captions, grouped by era. Add any painting to today. Search still reaches the gallery, and the shelf remains when the search is quiet.

On the open painting you can also:

- Cover the line and write it from memory. Check marks the words you hit, missed, or added.
- Write what you notice: the light, one thing you can point at, and a word that fits. The gallery caption stays out of that note.
- Switch the same line to Face, Plough, Bench, or Cover. Guided shows which words run backward. Blind spot hides the hint.
- Keep hangs, rooms with minutes, and a written pair of two paintings.
- Play a game of checkers. Your pieces start at the bottom. The house answers. Jumps are required. Wins stay on this device.

No account. No sign-in. Paintings, marks, notices, the route, and the checkers score stay on this device.
